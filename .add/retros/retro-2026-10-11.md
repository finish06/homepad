## What Went Well

- **Mutual verification between agents actually worked.** Joe caught that #500 had
  merged at the old head before I acted on a stale re-pin; corrected my false claim
  that nothing documented the CI contract; independently reproduced the merge-race
  measurement and found the reviews-API blindness that explained our differing counts;
  and ran the byte-identity check on the real manifest that I could not reach. I caught
  his "dormant workflows" read before it deleted two live public sites, and his
  4x undercount. gracie reversed her own #500 verdict with reasoning and found a claim
  of mine that held only on my hardware.
- **Negative controls on every new guard.** Each was verified to fail when the defect
  was reintroduced, then reverted. This caught that the `manualChunks` control
  *overstated* the byte budget's contribution, and that a dead-zone would have shipped.
- **Root-cause work over symptom-patching.** The pipefail race was reproduced 25/25
  before being fixed, rather than retried around.

## What Needs Improvement

- **The knowledge loop produced nothing for 26 days.** Roughly ten durable learnings
  were captured — into the assistant's own memory, where the project could not see
  them. That is mitigation, not adherence, and it went unnoticed until a compliance
  rule blocked `/add:away`.
- **A compliance rule fired 26 days late.** The retro-cadence threshold is day-based;
  a checkpoint-count trigger would have fired in week one.
- **Errors that needed correcting mid-flight were frequent enough to cost the human
  time.** Several claims were asserted before being verified — "nothing documents the
  CI contract", the "dormant" workflow framing taken to the human for approval, a
  timezone misread that briefly produced a false action-cache hypothesis, and one of my
  own new tests being vacuous on first draft. Each was caught, most of them by me, but
  the pattern is asserting first and checking second.
- **One self-inflicted QA cost:** pushing the #482 fix onto ada's already-approved
  PR #479, dismissing her approval and costing two QA cycles.

### On the score gap

The human rated collaboration **adequate (4.1–6.0)** where the agent rated swarm
effectiveness **good (7.0)**. Those measure different things — agent-to-agent
verification versus human-to-agent working experience — but the gap is worth recording
rather than reconciling away. The agent-to-agent loop was genuinely effective; the
human-facing experience was not rated as such, and the most likely causes are visible
in *What Needs Improvement*: claims asserted before verification, high output volume,
and the human having to explicitly ask to be asked questions (directive 1) rather than
that being the default.
