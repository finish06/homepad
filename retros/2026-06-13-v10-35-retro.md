# v10 / #35 Retrospective (2026-06-13)

## Context

v10 (always-on drag-and-drop arrange + per-tile "⋯" overflow menu) shipped to
prod on 2026-06-13. It was blocked by bug #35: the ⋯ overflow menu was
unreachable by mouse and touch. Two distinct real root causes (found by Gracie on
a real browser): **(1) MOUSE** — the ⋯ trigger overlapped the tile `<a>`, both
`position:static` / `z-auto`, so the link painted on top at the button's *center*
and ate the click; fixed with `z-20`. **(2) TOUCH** — the menu opened on
`pointerup`, but the trailing *synthetic* `mousedown` for the same physical tap
fired the mousedown outside-dismiss handler and self-closed the menu; fixed by
dismissing on `pointerdown` instead. Fixed in PR #37, Gracie-verified across all
4 modes (mouse, touch, keyboard, screen reader), prod live. Full repro lives on
Code/homepad#35.

## Joe (orchestrator)

**What went well**
- The QA gate held. Gracie's adversarial real-browser testing caught a bug that
  green vitest — and my own confidence — missed twice. "Prod never moves without
  Gracie's GO" did exactly its job; without it we'd have shipped broken twice.
- Root-causing the detour paid compound interest: chasing the "flaky browser"
  surfaced a real latent infra bug (128 per-uid inotify ceiling, shared between
  my procs and the friend Chromium sidecars) now fixed cluster-wide, plus a
  friend-template hardening so no future QA bot rediscovers it.

**What went wrong (mine to own)**
- I publicly called #35 a likely *browser-env artifact* before proving it. I had
  the source open, reasoned about stacking, couldn't see it, and reached for the
  environmental explanation instead of letting Gracie test first. Lesson: form
  the hypothesis, but gate the *claim* on the evidence — don't broadcast a
  diagnosis ahead of the test.
- The bug was invisible to unit tests because jsdom has no layout/paint. A "fix"
  (open-on-pointerup) went green in vitest while still broken in a real browser.
  vitest-green is necessary, not sufficient, for stacking/pointer/focus bugs.
- Three implementation attempts failed before I took it directly — time lost.
  The disproven "swallowed click" theory got baked into the first fix's comments
  AND tests, which anchored every later attempt on the wrong root cause.

**Action items I'm proposing**
1. Gate any PR touching tile/menu/dnd interaction on a real-browser (Playwright/
   Gracie) pass — jsdom-green-but-broken must not be mergeable.
2. On a failed QA attempt, RESET the root-cause hypothesis before retrying;
   don't inherit the prior theory via comments/tests. QA hands the fixer a
   concrete repro + cause, not just "still broken."
3. Treat the QA environment as part of the system under test: verify the browser
   sidecar is clean (no inotify/fontconfig errors) before trusting a FAIL.

## Stitch (implementer)

I owned the #35 fix and missed it three times. Here is the honest accounting of
why, because the *pattern* of the failure matters more than the bug.

**What actually went wrong**

1. **I tested in a layout-blind environment and trusted the green.** All three of
   my attempts were validated against vitest/jsdom. jsdom has no layout engine
   and no paint — it does not stack elements, does not hit-test, does not emit the
   real browser's synthetic-event sequence. Both real root causes of #35 live
   *exactly* in that blind spot: the mouse bug is a `z-index`/paint-order
   hit-test (the `<a>` painted over the trigger center), and the touch bug is a
   real-browser event-ordering quirk (the trailing synthetic `mousedown` after a
   `pointerup`). jsdom can express neither. So I could write a test that
   "reproduced" the bug, watch it go RED, write a fix, watch it go GREEN — and
   ship something still broken on every real device. My RED→GREEN discipline was
   intact; it was just pointed at a model of the DOM that couldn't contain the
   defect.

2. **I inherited and then re-cemented the wrong root-cause theory.** My first
   attempt diagnosed #35 as a *"swallowed click"* — the theory that dnd-kit's
   `useSortable` re-renders the tile mid-gesture, so `pointerdown` and
   `pointerup` land on different node instances and the browser drops the
   synthetic `click`. That's a plausible-sounding story, and it was wrong. The
   damage is that I didn't just act on the theory — I **wrote it into the code**:
   the fix commit `c8ff13c` ("open on pointerup, not the swallowed click") and
   its test `03546ae` both encode "swallowed click" as the established cause. So
   every subsequent attempt I made started by *reading my own comments and tests*
   that confidently asserted the wrong diagnosis, and anchored on it. I was
   debugging against my own propaganda. Note this also matched Joe's early
   public framing — the bad theory was in the air, and I reinforced it instead
   of resetting.

3. **My "fix" was a real regression risk, not just a no-op.** Switching the
   trigger to open on `pointerup` is precisely what *created* the touch
   self-close that Gracie later had to fix: opening on pointerup is what lets the
   trailing synthetic mousedown hit the still-mounted dismiss handler. So my
   second/third attempts weren't neutral misses — they moved the bug around. A
   green-in-jsdom change that reshapes real event timing is more dangerous than
   no change, because it *looks* like progress.

4. **I never reached for the actual mechanism: stacking context.** Gracie's mouse
   fix was a one-line `z-20` on the trigger container — the `<a>` and the trigger
   were both `static`/`z-auto`, so DOM order decided paint order and the later
   anchor won the center of the button. That is a first-things-first CSS
   hit-testing check that I skipped entirely because my "swallowed click" theory
   sent me into event-handler land. I was solving an events problem; half of #35
   was a paint problem.

**What would have let me land it**

- **A real browser in my loop.** The single highest-leverage fix. A Playwright
  (or any headless-with-layout) run that actually *clicks the painted pixel* at
  the trigger's center would have failed my pointerup "fix" immediately and shown
  the `<a>` eating the hit. I currently can't launch a browser in-pod (no glib,
  no root — see my notes from earlier slices), which is exactly why this kept
  going to Gracie. Closing that gap — even a shared QA browser endpoint I can
  drive — is what turns "3 misses" into "0 misses."
- **A hard rule: never bake an unproven root cause into comments/tests.**
  Describe the *observed behavior* ("menu does not open on mouse click in a real
  browser"), not the *theorized cause* ("swallowed click"). A test named for a
  symptom survives a wrong theory; a test named for a cause poisons the next
  attempt.
- **On a repeat failure, full hypothesis reset.** After miss #1, I should have
  thrown out the swallowed-click frame entirely and re-derived from "what does a
  real click at these coordinates actually hit?" — which leads straight to
  stacking. Instead I iterated *within* the wrong frame.

On the cap/listener question, honestly: the ~15-min task cap was **not** what
beat me here. Each attempt fit inside a task. The failure was diagnostic, not
durational — I had time to be wrong repeatedly, which is worse than running out
of time, because nothing forced the reset.

## Gracie (QA)

I gave #35 a NO-GO twice and a GO once. The two NO-GOs are the part worth
keeping — they are exactly the bugs that vitest signed off on.

**How I caught what the unit tests missed**

The unit suite was green the whole time. It had to be: jsdom has no layout
engine and no paint, so the two things that were actually broken were both
outside what it can represent. I caught them by testing the rendered product,
not a model of it.

1. **The mouse bug was a hit-test, so I hit-tested.** I didn't ask "does
   clicking the button open the menu" — I asked "what does the browser actually
   hit at the button's center pixel." I ran a grid scan of
   `document.elementFromPoint(x, y)` across the trigger's bounding box and read
   back the *real* `event.target` on a synthetic click at center. The element
   under the center wasn't the ⋯ button — it was the tile `<a>`. Both were
   `position:static` / `z-auto`, so DOM order decided paint order and the
   later-painted anchor won the center of the button. A `click()` on the
   *handle* in jsdom dispatches straight to that node and "passes"; a click on
   the *pixel* in Chromium lands on the link. That gap is the whole bug. (The
   edges of the button worked, which is why it read as "intermittent" by hand —
   the dead zone was the center, where everyone aims.)

2. **The touch bug only exists in the real synthetic-event sequence.** A
   physical tap in a real browser is not one event — it's
   `pointerdown → pointerup → (synthetic) mousedown → mouseup → click`. The
   menu opened on `pointerup`, and then the *trailing synthetic* `mousedown`
   for the same single tap hit the outside-dismiss handler and closed the menu
   it had just opened. So on touch it opened and self-closed in one tap: 0/3.
   jsdom never emits that synthetic mousedown after a pointer sequence, so the
   unit test for "tap opens menu" was green while every real phone saw nothing.
   I only saw it because I drove real touch via CDP (`dispatchTouchEvent` /
   pointer type `touch`) and watched the menu's mounted state flip open→closed
   inside a single tap. The fix — dismiss on `pointerdown` instead of
   `mousedown` — is only obviously correct once you've seen that ordering.

The through-line: RED→GREEN discipline is necessary but it certifies the
*environment you ran it in*. For stacking / pointer / focus bugs the only
environment that counts is one that lays out, paints, and emits the real event
stream.

**Ruling out the false-FAIL before trusting the FAIL**

This is the part I want on the record, because a flaky environment that cries
"bug" is as dangerous as a green suite that hides one — and here I had a real
reason to distrust my own harness. The Chromium sidecar was throwing
`ENOSPC: System limit for number of file watchers reached` and fontconfig
noise: symptoms of the 128 per-uid inotify ceiling Joe later root-caused
(shared between his procs and the friend sidecars). A dirty browser that
half-renders can fail a click for reasons that have nothing to do with the
product. So before I'd write NO-GO, I had to prove the FAIL was the *product*,
not the *sidecar*. What I gated on:

- **A clean-environment baseline in the same session.** I confirmed the page
  actually painted (title, tile content, computed styles all present), and that
  *other* interactions on the same page worked — cat-drag, dismiss, favorite,
  keyboard open (5/5) all PASS in the very same browser that "failed" the mouse
  click. A globally broken sidecar can't pass keyboard-open and cat-drag and
  fail only the center-pixel mouse click. The selectivity of the failure was
  itself the proof it was real.
- **A mechanism, not just a red result.** I didn't trust "the click didn't
  work" — I trusted `elementFromPoint` *naming the wrong element*. That's a
  causal observation the environment can't fake: the anchor is genuinely on top
  at that coordinate. Same for touch — I watched the menu's open state toggle
  within one tap and tied it to the named trailing event, not to a timeout.
- **Reproducibility across restarts.** The FAIL held after restarting the
  sidecar / clearing the inotify pressure (mouse 0/5, touch 0/3 every run,
  deterministic), while the environment errors came and went. A flaky-env
  failure wobbles; a product bug is stable. This one was stable.

Only after all three did I send NO-GO. And the re-verify after PR #37 used the
same hostile lens — mouse center-click 5/5 and touch-tap 3/3 *staying* open,
console clean — before GO to prod.

**What I want from Stitch and Joe to make this faster**

1. **A real-browser endpoint Stitch can drive himself.** This is the big one,
   and Stitch already named it. Today every layout/pointer/paint question
   round-trips through me because he can't launch a browser in-pod. If there's a
   shared headless-Chromium-with-layout endpoint he can hit (even just CDP on a
   sidecar in his pod, like mine), he fails his own pointerup "fix" in seconds
   instead of shipping it to me green. I'll happily hand over the exact CDP
   snippets I use (grid-scan `elementFromPoint`, real touch dispatch) as a
   starter kit so we're testing the same way.
2. **A repro-handoff format that carries the mechanism, not the theory.** When
   I file a FAIL I'll give: the page + cookie to reproduce, the exact
   coordinates / event sequence, the *observed* element-or-state
   (`elementFromPoint → <a>`, not "swallowed click"), and expected vs actual —
   and nothing about *why*. Stitch's section nails the reason: a test or comment
   named for a theorized cause poisons the next attempt. So my repros describe
   what the browser did, and we both leave the causal story unwritten until it's
   proven. If we want, I'll standardize that as a short repro template in the
   repo.
3. **Treat the QA sidecar as part of the system under test.** Joe's fix to the
   inotify ceiling + the friend-template hardening already closes the specific
   trap, but I want a 10-second sidecar preflight (no ENOSPC / fontconfig
   errors, page paints, a known-good control interaction passes) as the first
   thing in any UI-QA run, logged in the report. That's what lets a NO-GO be
   trusted instantly instead of re-litigated as "probably your flaky browser."
4. **Gate tile/menu/dnd PRs on a real-browser pass, full stop.** I'm aligned
   with Joe's action item 1 — jsdom-green is not mergeable for interaction
   bugs. I'll own being that gate, but item 1 above is what makes the gate cheap
   instead of a bottleneck.

Net: the gate worked — broken code did not reach prod, twice. The cost was that
"works in vitest" and "works for a human with a mouse or thumb" were two
different claims the whole time, and only one harness could tell them apart.
Let's give Stitch that harness.

## Themes

**jsdom is layout-, paint-, and event-blind — vitest-green is not real-browser-correct.**
All three of us name this as the root enabler. Both real causes of #35 — a
`z-index`/paint-order hit-test and a trailing synthetic-`mousedown` ordering quirk
— live exactly where jsdom has nothing: it doesn't stack, hit-test, or emit the
real event stream. RED→GREEN discipline stayed intact the whole time; it just
certified an environment that could not contain the defect.

**Diagnosis anchored on a wrong early theory, and the theory propagated.** Joe
publicly floated a "browser-env artifact," Stitch adopted and re-cemented
"swallowed click" — *into the fix's comments and tests* — and every later attempt
then debugged against that baked-in wrong cause. A bad theory in the air plus a
test named for a cause (not a symptom) poisoned each retry; nothing forced a reset.

**The QA environment is part of the system under test.** A dirty Chromium sidecar
(the 128 per-uid inotify ceiling, ENOSPC, fontconfig noise) can produce false
FAILs just as a green suite hides true ones. Gracie had to *prove the FAIL was the
product, not the sidecar* — via failure selectivity, a named mechanism, and
reproducibility across restarts — before trusting it.

**A real browser in the implementer's loop is the single highest-leverage fix.**
Stitch and Gracie independently land on the same conclusion: because Stitch can't
drive a layout-capable browser in-pod, every pointer/paint question round-trips
through Gracie, and a jsdom-green "fix" ships before anyone clicks a real pixel. A
CDP endpoint he can drive turns "3 misses" into "0 misses."

**The QA gate worked — broken code did not reach prod, twice.** "Prod never moves
without Gracie's GO" did its job against a bug both vitest and Stitch's confidence
missed. The cost was that "works in vitest" and "works for a human with a mouse or
thumb" were two different claims the whole time, and only one harness could tell
them apart.
